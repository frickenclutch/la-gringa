Chapter 1 · Guests

The guest’s path

Every guest walks the same short road: choose a language, pass the patio gate, pick a direction at the hub, open the manuscript. The Patio Board is a side door only you use. Tap a station to see what happens there.Each station is described in turn below.

Your side doorThe Patio Board/owner · chapter 3

The Passport · first visit on a device

The first time a phone or computer opens any page of the site, a passport slides up with three skillets: Español, English, Français. The choice is stamped into that browser and every page follows it from then on. There is no account and no cookie; the guest can switch at any time with the EN | ES | FR chip in the corner of every page.

What follows the choice: the gate text, the hub cards, the menu’s section titles, dish names and descriptions, buttons and hints. What stays exactly as printed: prices, the phone number, the address, the hours.

Decide what a code is worth

The manuscript is three things layered together: the printed menu, the live layer you edit from the board, and the atmosphere that changes with the time of day.

The Street Board

A red Specials button sits in the corner of every page of the book. The riverfront and the atmosphere

Behind the book is a painting of the Dobisky waterfront seen from the river, worked up from photographs of the site: the long low building with its silver roof, deep overhang and white rafter tails, full-height glass over a stacked-stone wall and the porch with its red picnic tables; the two-tier gazebo on the concrete pier at the right; the marina’s grey floating docks with a pontoon boat and a blue powerboat in front; green lantern lamps and benches along the promenade, the apartment tower behind the trees and the bridge far off to the left. It recolours with the guest’s own clock: day from 6 am to 5 pm, dusk from 5 to 8 pm, night after that. The moon in the sky is drawn in tonight’s real shape. The excursion boat ties up at the dock and makes trips out and back; papel picado hangs across the top; fireflies and a moth come out after dark.

A few dates change the weather: marigold petals fall for Día de los Muertos (Oct 25 – Nov 3), snow from December through February, confetti for Cinco de Mayo (May 1 – 6) and Independencia (Sep 15 – 16). Chapter 3 · Owners

The Patio Board

One private page runs everything that changes week to week: the month label on the cover, what’s new and gone, today’s specials, prices and wording on the printed pages, and your PIN.

Getting in

  1. Open dirtygringonny.com/owner, or open the menu and tap the wax seal on the cover five times quickly (within about two and a half seconds). The seal singes and the PIN gate appears.
  2. Type your PIN and press Unlock board. You stay signed in on that device for 12 hours; after that the gate comes back.
  3. When you are done on a shared device, press Sign out at the top. Back to the site returns to the hub.
Five wrong tries and the board rests

After five wrong PINs from the same connection the board refuses everything for 10 minutes, including the correct PIN. The counter is shared between the PIN gate, the setup screen and the Change PIN panel. If it happens without you trying five times, someone on the same Wi-Fi is guessing; change the PIN once you are in.

Daily specials

Add special adds a row. Each row has a Name (required), a Price typed the way you want it to read (the board prints exactly what you type: 14.00 or $14), a Note (the chalk line under the name), Starts and Ends dates, an Active box and a Remove button.

  • Active unchecked hides the special without deleting it. Handy for a Friday-only item you bring back every week.
  • Dates are inclusive and either may be blank. Blank on both sides means always on while Active is checked.
  • Remove only takes effect once you Save board. So does everything else on the page.
  • The server checks dates against the UTC calendar. In practice a special with an Ends date disappears around 8 pm Eastern that evening (7 pm in winter), and one with a Starts date shows up around 8 pm the evening before. You close at 7, so this rarely matters; if you post a late-night special, give it an extra day.

History

Every Save board adds a line: the month label, when it was saved, and how many additions, takeaways and specials it carried. The site keeps the last 40 saves. It is a record, not an undo button: to go back to an earlier board, re-enter the values and save again. Refresh reloads the list if you have the board open on two devices. Your developer can restore any of the 40 snapshots in full from storage if something is ever lost.

Menu Pages · editing the printed menu itself

Edit the menu manuscript opens the menu with the address /menu?edit=1. Every editable dish, price and description glows. Tap one and a parchment sheet slides up from the bottom:

  • Name and Description in the language the chip is set to (500 characters at most each).
  • The price fields for that item: usually one Price; Regular and Loaded for quesadillas; First column and Second column for the salsa sizes.
  • Save — goes live applies the change for every guest from their next load. Reset to original removes your change and the printed text comes back. Cancel closes the sheet.
  • What you just changed bleeds in like fresh ink for a moment so you can see it landed. Done in the top bar returns you to the board.

Your edits sit as a layer over the printed menu rather than replacing it, which is why Reset always works and why the printed menu is what guests see if the server is unreachable. Prices are shared by all three languages; names and descriptions are per language, and switching the EN | ES | FR chip while editing lets you write the Spanish or French side yourself (chapter 4 explains what the site translates for you).

Bounced back to the board?

The editor checks that you are signed in before it opens. If your 12 hours ran out, it sends you to the PIN gate; unlock and press the Menu Pages button again.

Change PIN

Enter the current PIN and a new one of at least six characters, then Update PIN. A short phrase beats a number: river-tacos-7 is far harder to guess than 123456 and just as easy to remember. Changing the PIN signs out every other device that was using the old one.

Lost the PIN? There is no reset link, on purpose. Your developer clears the stored PIN and sends you a fresh one-time setup link, valid for seven days. Open it, choose a new PIN, and you are back in. Nothing on the board is lost in the process.

The PIN is the only lock

The site’s code is public and the five-tap seal is a convenience, not a secret. Treat the PIN like the key to the register: don’t paste it in group chats, and sign out on devices that aren’t yours.

Chapter 4 · Owners

Three languages

The site speaks English, Spanish and Canadian French. The printed menu was translated once, by hand. Your edits are translated for you, with one firm rule: the machine never overwrites what a person typed.

How guests get their language

The passport on the first visit sets it; the EN | ES | FR chip changes it. Around two hundred pieces of interface text exist in each language, from the gate’s welcome to the install card. Everything a guest reads follows the choice except the figures: prices, the phone number, the address and the hours are the same in every language.

How your menu edits get translated

When you save a name or description in one language, the site asks Cloudflare’s translation model for the other two, using an instruction tuned for a Mexican menu. The toast reads Saved — other languages translated automatically when that worked, or Saved — guests see it now when the translator was unavailable and only your language changed. Prices never go through translation.

The ownership rule decides what gets translated. Each language of each item is either printed (nothing changed yet), yours (a person typed it in the editor) or machine (filled from your text in another language). When you save a fresh edit, every sibling language that is printed or machine-owned is retranslated from your new text; a sibling that is yours is left alone. To fix a translation you don’t like, switch the chip to that language, tap the item and rewrite it: it becomes yours and stays put.

Chapter 5 · Administrators

Under the hood

The whole site is one Cloudflare Worker with a folder of static files behind it. There is no database server, no CMS and nothing that needs patching; the moving parts are listed here so nothing is a mystery on the day someone has to look.

Where it runs

The Worker is named la-gringas and lives in the Cloudflare account called The Dirty Gringo. The pages, scripts, fonts and the riverfront painting are uploaded with it as static assets and served from Cloudflare’s edge. A small program runs in front of every request: it sends the old addresses and old WordPress links to the right page on dirtygringonny.com, answers every address that starts with /api/ (promo codes for the game, the public board, the owner’s tools), and passes everything else through to the static files.

What is stored, and where

Everything the owner changes lives in one Cloudflare KV namespace bound as MENU_BOARD. Each key is a small JSON document:

KeyHolds
currentThe board: month label, year, notes, additions, takeaways, specials.
historyThe last 40 board snapshots, newest first.
menu-overridesThe live layer over the printed menu: per item, any of price, regular, loaded, p1, p2, name and description in en/es/fr, plus a server-derived list of which fields are machine-translated.
menu-historyThe last 20 override documents, for restoring an edit.
owner-authThe owner’s PIN as a salted PBKDF2 hash and the secret that signs sessions. Delete this key to reset the PIN.
owner-claim-tokenThe one-time setup token, present only while a setup link is outstanding (7-day expiry).
login-fails:<ip>The per-connection failure counter behind the 10-minute lockout.

If KV is unreachable the public board falls back to the seed in data/menu-board.json, and the printed menu shows with no overrides. Guests never see an error, only older content.

Translation

The Worker has a Workers AI binding. Saves go to @cf/meta/llama-3.3-70b-instruct-fp8-fast with a menu-specific system prompt; if that model fails, @cf/meta/m2m100-1.2b is tried; if both fail, the save still succeeds untranslated. Ownership of each field is derived on the server by comparing the new document with the stored one, so nothing a browser sends can mark human text as machine text.

What guests do and don’t leave behind

  • No accounts, no cookies, no analytics tag and no third-party scripts on any guest page.
  • Three preferences stay on the guest’s own device: the language (dg-lang), the page-turn sound (dg-sound) and whether the page-corner hint has played (dg-flip-hint).
  • The owner gets one HttpOnly, same-site cookie (dg_owner) that expires after 12 hours and is cleared by Sign out.
  • Traffic numbers, if wanted, come from Cloudflare’s cookieless Web Analytics, switched on in the dashboard rather than in the code.

The offline cache

A service worker (sw.js, version dg-v21) makes the installed app work on weak signal. Pages are fetched network-first, so a guest online always gets fresh HTML and the cache is only a fallback. Scripts, styles, fonts and data are served from cache while a fresh copy downloads in the background, so a code deploy can take a second visit to show on a device that already had the menu open. The board and menu APIs are never cached: owner edits are always live on the next load. Bumping the version string forces every device onto a clean cache at its next visit. A copy of the worker installed from one of the old addresses retires itself, so an app added to the home screen from the workers.dev address simply lands on the domain; adding it again from dirtygringonny.com gives it the proper address.

Security posture, in short

  • The PIN is stored hashed; sessions are signed with a secret that rotates when the PIN changes, which is what signs out other devices.
  • Login, setup and PIN change share a five-failure, ten-minute lockout per connection. A Cloudflare WAF rate rule on /api/owner/* is a cheap extra belt if the board ever draws attention.
  • Owner writes are sanitised against a field whitelist with size caps (400 items, 500 characters per field) and the promo codes exist only in the Worker, never in the browser code.
  • The repository on GitHub (frickenclutch/la-gringa) is public. No PIN, token or account secret is in it, and none should ever be committed.

Chapter 6 · Administrators

Deploying and checking

Deploys are deliberate and manual. Pushing to GitHub refreshes the old mirror’s redirect and nothing else; the live site changes only when someone runs the deploy command from a machine signed in to the Cloudflare account.

The commands

npm install          # once per machine
npm test             # repo smoke tests: structure, languages, service worker, no leaked codes
npm run test:mobile  # Playwright on iPhone, Galaxy, Fold and desktop profiles
npm run dev          # local preview with the real APIs at http://localhost:8787
npm run deploy       # build CSS, stage dist/, upload the Worker and its assets

Local development reads .dev.vars for a throwaway OWNER_PIN; production has no PIN secret at all, because the owner set theirs through the site.

Deploy-day checklist

Ticks are remembered on this device only.

Domain day: dirtygringonny.com is on the site (done 11 September 2026)

The zone moved into the Dirty Gringo Cloudflare account, the bare domain was bound to the Worker and Cloudflare issued the certificate; the WordPress host and its expired certificate are out of the picture. What is left to tidy, and what to check:

Resetting a lost PIN

  1. Delete the owner-auth key from the MENU_BOARD namespace (dashboard, or npx wrangler kv key delete --binding MENU_BOARD --remote owner-auth).
  2. Seed a fresh one-time token and send the owner the link it prints. The README carries the exact command; it stores owner-claim-token with a seven-day expiry and echoes /owner?claim=… on the live address.
  3. The owner opens the link, chooses a new PIN, and lands in the editor. The token burns on use. Board content and menu edits are untouched throughout.
Machine quirk worth knowing

A stale CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN in a Windows user environment overrides the dashboard login and makes every Wrangler command fail with an auth error. Remove the variable, or prefix commands with env -u CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN in a bash shell.

Chapter 7 · Everyone

When something looks wrong

Start from what you see. Most of these fix themselves in a minute; the rest tell you exactly who to call and what to say.

The PIN page says “Owner tools don’t run on this address” or shows a “read-only copy” panel

Old bookmarks to la-gringa.pages.dev or the workers.dev address now forward to the domain on their own, so this should no longer happen. If it does, you are looking at a saved or offline copy of the page: press Open the real Patio Board or type dirtygringonny.com/owner, and replace the bookmark.

“Too many attempts — the board is resting”

Five wrong PINs from your connection. Wait ten minutes; the correct PIN is refused too until then. If you did not make those attempts, someone on the same network did: change the PIN once you are in, and tell your developer if it keeps happening.

I forgot the PIN

There is no reset link. Ask your developer for a fresh setup link (chapter 6 has the procedure). It arrives as a private address ending in /owner?claim=…, works once, and expires after seven days. Choose a phrase you will remember this time and note it somewhere safe.

My phone only offers a number keypad in the PIN box

The login box currently asks phones for a numeric keypad. A PIN made of digits types fine; a PIN with letters cannot be entered on an iPhone until your developer makes the one-line fix in the login form. Until then use a computer, or a phone keyboard that lets you switch to letters.

I saved a special but I don’t see it on the menu

Check three things on the board: the Active box is ticked, the Starts date is today or earlier (or blank), the Ends date is today or later (or blank). Then reload the menu: pull down in the browser, or close and reopen the installed app. The Street Board fetches the live board every time the menu opens.

The cover shows an old month, or “August 2026”

The cover shows the Month label from the board. If you changed it and saved, reload the menu. “August 2026” is the built-in fallback: if the board says something else but the cover still shows August, the menu could not reach the server. tell your developer, and mention whether the Street Board also shows the built-in specials (Birria Quesatacos and River Fish Taco Plate).

The game says “Show this win at the counter” instead of a code

The game could not reach the server for a code: the guest is on the old mirror address, offline, or the site is down. Honor the win at your discretion and ask them to try the live address. If it happens on the live address with a good connection, the Worker is not answering; contact your developer.

A Spanish or French dish name reads wrong

Open Menu Pages, switch the EN | ES | FR chip to that language, tap the item and rewrite it. From then on that text is yours and the translator leaves it alone (chapter 4).

I changed the English name and the Spanish one didn’t follow

Someone typed that Spanish text by hand earlier, so it is protected from the machine. Edit it directly in Spanish, or, to hand it back to the translator, tell your developer to clear that field.

“Saved” appeared but guests still see the old price

The change is live on the server. A menu that was already open keeps its page until it reloads; the installed app should be fully closed and reopened. If a fresh load on a different phone still shows the old price, tell your developer which item.

Menu Pages bounces me back to the board

Your 12-hour session ended or you were never signed in on this device. Unlock the board again, then press Edit the menu manuscript.

www.dirtygringonny.com shows the home page instead of the menu, or a “522” error

The bare domain has been on the new site since 11 September 2026 with a certificate Cloudflare renews itself. The www spelling is not on the site yet: over https it lands on the home page whatever page was asked for, and over plain http it fails with a 522 error. Until the fix in chapter 6 (Domain day) is done, share and print dirtygringonny.com without the www.

The menu feels slow or stutters on a phone

Phones already get the lighter version with the moving effects off. Close other tabs and apps, and try a fresh load. If it is still sluggish, note the phone model and browser and tell your developer; the remaining effects can be dialled down further.

“Get the Menu App” never shows up

It hides once the app is installed. On an iPhone it only works in Safari, so a link opened inside Facebook, Instagram or Chrome will not offer it: ask the guest to open the address in Safari. Some desktop browsers cannot install web apps at all.

The Street Board says “No specials posted right now”

Nothing is active for today: every special is unticked, has not started, or has ended. Fix the dates or tick Active and save. If you are sure something is active, see “The cover shows an old month” above.

No page-turn sound

The speaker button beside the language chip is off (the choice is remembered per device), or the phone’s silent switch is on. Sound only plays on real page turns, not while the book is loading.

Can I undo a save on the board?

Not from the board: History is a record. Re-enter the earlier values and save. Your developer can restore any of the last 40 board snapshots or the last 20 menu-edit snapshots from storage.

The passport shows every single time I open the site

The browser is refusing to remember anything: a private window, or site data being cleared on close. Open the site in a normal window. Guests who see this are in the same situation and lose nothing but the one tap.

Nothing matches that. Try one word, or read the list without a filter.

Gate
The front page with the skillet game.
Hub
The crossroads page with the four cards and the hours.
Manuscript
The menu, presented as a book.
Street Board
The chalk overlay on the menu with today’s specials and This month.
Patio Board
The owner’s private page at /owner.
Menu Pages
Edit mode on the manuscript itself, at /menu?edit=1.
Override
One of your edits, stored as a layer over the printed menu.
Session
The 12 hours a device stays unlocked after a correct PIN.
Lockout
The 10-minute rest after five wrong PINs from one connection.
Setup link
The one-time /owner?claim=… address used to choose a PIN.
Worker
Cloudflare’s name for the small program that runs the site at the edge.
KV
Cloudflare’s key-value storage, where the board and your edits live.
Workers AI
Cloudflare’s hosted models, used here only for translating your edits.
Mirror
The old la-gringa.pages.dev address, now just a forwarder.
Lite mode
The lighter menu that phones and reduced-motion devices get.

PathWhat it is
index.html · js/gate-game.jsThe gate and the skillet game. Recipes come from data/recipes.json; the promo codes live only in worker.js.
hub.htmlThe hub. Its hours text comes from data/i18n.json (the hub.hours… strings) and its search-engine data is inline.
menu.htmlThe printed menu and the book’s styling. Scripts: menu-book.js (page turns), menu-board-ui.js (Street Board, cover month, the five-tap seal), menu-live.js (applies overrides), menu-edit.js (edit mode), menu-ambience.js (riverfront, seasons, moon, sound), menu-install.js (Get the Menu App).
owner.html · js/owner-board.jsThe Patio Board, including the setup screen and Change PIN.
js/i18n.js · data/i18n.jsonThe passport, the chip and all interface strings in three languages. tools/build-i18n-fr.mjs regenerates missing French strings.
data/site.jsonVenue facts for search engines and the app manifest: address, phone, hours, map query. Hours therefore live here, in i18n.json and in hub.html; change all three together.
data/menu-board.jsonThe fallback board shown when the server cannot be reached.
worker.js · wrangler.jsoncEvery /api/ route, the sanitisers, translation and auth, plus the redirects that send old addresses and old WordPress links to the domain; and the Cloudflare configuration (Worker name, the complete list of custom domains, KV binding, AI binding).
_redirectsInstructions for the old Pages mirror only: forward everything to the domain, and keep serving the service worker so old installs can retire themselves. Never copied into dist/.
sw.jsThe offline cache. Bump VERSION to flush every device.
art/riverfront.svgThe waterfront painting; colours are variables set per time of day in menu.html.
tests/ · tools/The smoke test, the Playwright suite and its static server; build scripts for fonts, icons and staging.
docs/how-the-site-works.htmlThis manual. It is not part of the deployed site; share the file directly. Its source, the screenshot script and the captured images live in docs/src/; after editing guide-body.html there, run node docs/src/build-guide.mjs from the repository root to rebuild this file.
docs/how-the-site-works.pdfA print of this manual for sharing on messengers and phones; the try-it widgets become worked examples. Regenerate with node docs/src/build-pdf.mjs after rebuilding the HTML.
